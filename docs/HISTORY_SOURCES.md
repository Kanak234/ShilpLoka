# ShilpLoka (Java Edition) — History Sources & Codex Guidelines

This document records the historical, archaeological, and architectural sources underpinning the structures, items, and codex entries in ShilpLoka's **Itihaas Granth** (history codex).

In adherence to Section 13 (Cultural Content Rules) and Section 2.11 of the Master Build Brief:
- All historical entries must be **factually accurate, respectful, and neutral**.
- Religious sites are generated as **peaceful landmarks**.
- Deities are **never** depicted as enemies, loot, or killable mobs.
- Historical commentary excludes present-day politics, religious controversies, and community stereotypes.
- Texts are referenced from reputable historical, archaeological, and scholastic institutions.

---

## 1. Core Reference Sources

1. **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)**:
   - Official excavation reports for Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Kalibangan.
   - Architectural surveys of temples (Nagara, Dravida, and Vesara styles).
   - Monograph series on Stepwells (*Baolis* / *Vavs* of Gujarat and Rajasthan).
2. **National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT)**:
   - *An Introduction to Indian Art (Part I & II)* — Central themes in Indian sculpture, temple architecture, and metalcraft.
   - Secondary history textbooks covering ancient, medieval, and early modern India.
3. **Scholarly & Academic Works**:
   - Upinder Singh, *A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India: From the Stone Age to the 12th Century*.
   - A.L. Basham, *The Wonder That Was India*.
   - George Michell, *The Hindu Temple: An Introduction to Its Meaning and Forms*.
   - Percy Brown, *Indian Architecture (Buddhist and Hindu Periods / Islamic Period)*.
   - Sharada Srinivasan, *Wootz Steel: An Advanced Material of the Ancient World* (National Institute of Advanced Studies, Bengaluru).

## 2. Eras & Architectural Typologies

### 2.1 Sindhu-Saraswati (Harappan Civilization, ~2600–1900 BCE)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Grid-pattern town layout with north-south/east-west arterial streets.
  - The Great Bath (*Mahasnanghar*): Waterproofed with natural bitumen/asphalt mortar, fired brick masonry, drainage conduits.
  - Public Granary (*Annagar*): Elevated brick platform with air ventilation channels to preserve grains.
  - Stepwell / Reservoir (inspired by Dholavira's rock-cut water management system).
* **Blocks & Items**: Fired terracotta brick (standard 1:2:4 ratio), bitumen mortar, alluvial mud, copper flat axes, steatite seals, carnelian beads.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Harappan towns were among the world's earliest planned urban centers, characterized by orthogonal street grids, standardized kiln-fired bricks, and subterranean masonry drainage systems."
  - *HI*: "हड़प्पा नगर विश्व के प्राचीनतम नियोजित शहरी केंद्रों में से थे, जो समकोण सड़कों, मानकीकृत पकी ईंटों और भूमिगत जल निकासी प्रणाली के लिए जाने जाते हैं।"

---

### 2.2 Maurya Era (~322–185 BCE)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Great Stupa complex (hemispherical brick dome, circumambulation path *pradakshina patha*, stone balustrades).
  - Monolithic Sandstone Pillar: Polished Chunar sandstone pillar featuring a generic animal capital (original stylized design, never reproducing the modern state emblem).
  - Rock-cut meditation chambers (inspired by Barabar caves).
* **Blocks & Items**: Polished sandstone, carved stone railings, copper punch-marked coins (*karshapana*), Maurya stone tablets.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "The Mauryan period was marked by monumental stone craftsmanship, characterized by mirror-polished Chunar sandstone pillars and the expansion of brick stupa architecture."
  - *HI*: "मौर्य काल स्मारकीय प्रस्तर कला के लिए प्रसिद्ध था, जिसमें दर्पण जैसी चमक वाले चुनार बलुआ पत्थर के स्तंभ और ईंटों के स्तूप प्रमुख थे।"

---

### 2.3 Gupta Era (~319–543 CE)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Early rock-hewn and structural stone temples (flat-roofed sanctum *garbhagriha*, pillared portico *mandapa*, intricate doorway carvings).
  - Rust-resistant Iron Monolith (monolithic forged pillar demonstrating early Indian metallurgy).
* **Blocks & Items**: Carved sandstone blocks, wrought iron billets, early Devanagari script scrolls, gold dinars.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Classical Gupta architecture established the structural stone temple form, while contemporaneous metallurgical advances produced forge-welded iron monoliths with exceptional corrosion resistance."
  - *HI*: "गुप्त काल में संरचनात्मक पाषाण मंदिरों के मूल स्वरूप की स्थापना हुई और असाधारण जंग-रोधी गढ़ा लोहे के स्तंभों जैसी धातु शिल्प कला का विकास हुआ।"

---

### 2.4 Chola Dynasty (~848–1279 CE)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Dravida style temple complex: Multi-tiered pyramid tower (*vimana*), monumental gateway towers (*gopurams*), enclosed pillared courtyards, granite base (*upapitha*).
  - Water reservoir (*Kalyani* / temple tank) with stepped granite margins.
* **Blocks & Items**: Dressed granite blocks, bronze alloy castings (lost-wax *cire perdue* method), carved granite pillars.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Chola architecture is celebrated for monumental granite *vimanas* and refined bronze casting via the lost-wax method, creating lasting works of South Indian stone and metal craft."
  - *HI*: "चोल वास्तुकला अपने विशाल ग्रेनाइट विमानों और मोम-पिघलाव विधि (लॉस्ट-वैक्स) द्वारा निर्मित उत्कृष्ट कांस्य मूर्तिकला के लिए विख्यात है।"

---

### 2.5 Vijayanagara Empire (~1336–1646 CE)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Boulder-strewn urban ruins: Monolithic stone chariot shrine, musical pillared halls (*Kalyana Mandapa*), public elephant stables with arched domes.
  - Sophisticated canal networks carving granite channels from the Tungabhadra river.
* **Blocks & Items**: Massive granite ashlar blocks, chlorite schist inlays, copper land-grant plates, gold *varahas*.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Vijayanagara master builders harmonized massive granite outcrops into fortified urban citadels, intricate stone pavilions, and grand ceremonial chariot structures."
  - *HI*: "विजयनगर के शिल्पकारों ने प्राकृतिक ग्रेनाइट शिलाखंडों को भव्य नगर परकोटों, अलंकृत मंडपों और अद्वितीय पाषाण रथ संरचनाओं में रूपांतरित किया।"

---

### 2.6 Rajput & Maratha Hill Forts (~14th–18th Century)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Natural contour-following hill fortress (*Durg*): Bastioned perimeter walls, *chhatri* lookouts, stepped water tanks (*baolis*), fortified gateways (*pol*), *jharokha* balconies.
* **Blocks & Items**: Yellow Jaisalmer sandstone, Makrana white marble, red sandstone, Wootz steel talwar blades, brass cannons.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Indian hill fortresses utilized natural rugged topography to construct formidable layered defensive bastions, stone *chhatri* pavilions, and self-sufficient rainwater harvesting stepwells."
  - *HI*: "पहाड़ी दुर्गों ने प्राकृतिक स्थलाकृति का उपयोग करते हुए सुदृढ़ सुरक्षा प्राचीरों, सुंदर छतरियों और वर्षा जल संचयन के लिए विशाल बावड़ियों का निर्माण किया।"

---

### 2.7 Indo-Islamic Architectural Heritage (~12th–17th Century)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Symmetrical geometric garden pavilions (*Charbagh*): Four-part axial waterways, domed central pavilions, decorative *jaali* (stone latticework) screens, floral pietra dura inlays.
* **Blocks & Items**: White marble, red sandstone, geometric glazed ceramic tiles, carved jaali panels, lime mortar.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Indo-Islamic structural heritage introduced monumental true arches, ribbed domes, geometrical jaali panels, and symmetrical four-quadrant garden (*charbagh*) water channels."
  - *HI*: "भारतीय-इस्लामी वास्तुकला ने वास्तविक मेहराबों, गुंबदों, सूक्ष्म जाली नक्काशी और सममित चारबाग जल प्रणालियों का सुंदर समन्वय प्रस्तुत किया।"

---

### 2.8 Colonial-Era Industrial & Coastal Heritage (~19th Century)
* **Archetype Structures**:
  - Abandoned brick railway viaducts, coastal stone lighthouses, narrow-gauge track cuts across mountain ghats.
* **Blocks & Items**: Industrial wrought iron beams, terracotta roof tiles, brass lanterns, telegraph wire coils.
* **Neutral Historical Codex Text**:
  - *EN*: "Nineteenth-century industrial infrastructure introduced dressed-stone railway bridges, mountain viaducts, and coastal beacon lighthouses along the Indian subcontinent's trade routes."
  - *HI*: "उन्नीसवीं शताब्दी के औद्योगिक विकास ने भारतीय उपमहाद्वीप में पाषाण रेलवे पुलों, पर्वतीय वायडक्ट्स और तटीय प्रकाशस्तंभों का निर्माण किया।"

## 3. Metallurgy & Craft Tradition Sources

### Wootz Steel (*Ukku*)
* **Origin**: Southern Indian crucible steel making tradition (documented in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Telangana from ~300 BCE onward).
* **Crafting Mechanism in ShilpLoka**:
  - Iron ore + charcoal + specific wood leaves sealed inside a closed terracotta crucible, heated to intense temperature in a stone *Bhatti* (forge).
  - Results in high-carbon steel with distinctive undulating water-pattern surface banding (Damascus pattern).
* **References**: Dr. Sharada Srinivasan and S. Ranganathan, *India's Legendary Wootz Steel: An Advanced Material of the Ancient World* (2004).
